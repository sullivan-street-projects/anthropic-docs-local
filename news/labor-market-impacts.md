# Labor Market Impacts of AI: A New Measure and Early Evidence

**Publication Date:** March 5, 2026

## Key Findings

Anthropic researchers introduced "observed exposure," a metric combining theoretical LLM capabilities with real-world usage patterns. The measure weights automated implementations and work-related applications more heavily than augmentative uses.

The analysis reveals that actual AI deployment significantly lags theoretical potential. Computer programmers show 75% coverage, while 30% of workers have zero exposure. Occupations with higher exposure face weaker employment projections through 2034.

Workers in highly exposed roles tend to be older, female, more educated, and earn substantially more—47% higher average wages than unexposed workers.

## Employment Impact Assessment

Examining unemployment trends since ChatGPT's release in late 2022, researchers found no systematic increase in joblessness among highly exposed workers. However, they detected suggestive evidence that hiring of young workers (ages 22-25) into exposed occupations has slowed by approximately half a percentage point monthly.

The framework established by this research can theoretically detect differential unemployment changes of about one percentage point. The authors acknowledge that job market impacts may resemble the gradual disruption of internet adoption or China trade effects rather than sudden COVID-like shocks.

## Methodology

The researchers combined three data sources: the O*NET occupational database, Anthropic's usage analytics, and Eloundou et al.'s theoretical capability assessments. Tasks rated as theoretically feasible by LLMs comprised 97% of observed Claude usage, validating the correlation between theoretical possibility and practical implementation.
