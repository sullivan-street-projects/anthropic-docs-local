# Investing in the public benefit

Anthropic, **October 18, 2026**. Anthropic is committing a portion of its shares to the **Anthropic Public Benefit Fund**, a new wholly owned entity whose stake grows as the company does.

## The commitment

Subject to stockholder approval, **5% of Anthropic's shares** will be committed immediately, and the board can add **2% for every 50% increase** in company value above a set baseline. Combined with the co-founders' pledge to donate 80% of their equity and an employee equity-donation match program (about half of employees participate), roughly **20% of Anthropic's shares** will be dedicated to the public good — plus any additional amount tied to valuation milestones.

## What stays inside the company vs. the Fund

Most of Anthropic's public benefit work — AI safety and interpretability research, applied biomedical research, and discounted Claude access for nonprofits and scientific institutions — **stays inside the company** because it also has for-profit value. The **Fund** will have more flexibility to pursue the mission in new ways, such as large donations to traditional nonprofits or direct distributions to specific populations. Labor-market impacts are one example, where Anthropic has already committed $200 million and the Fund may take bolder approaches.

## Structure

The Fund is organized as an **LLC** rather than a 501(c)(3) to allow adjustable forms of giving. None of its Anthropic equity will be used for **lobbying or political giving**. Anthropic frames this as redirecting equity that tech companies typically award to executives as escalating compensation toward the public benefit. It says it will share more on the Fund's structure, governance, and milestones in the coming months, along with a public benefit report on its progress.
