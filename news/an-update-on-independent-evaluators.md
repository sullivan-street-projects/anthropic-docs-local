# An update on independent evaluators

Anthropic, **October 15, 2026**. Anthropic has begun giving **"employee-like access"** to a team from METR, a nonprofit that evaluates frontier AI models — following its commitment to stronger independent third-party assessment and its signing of the White House Accord on Super Intelligence.

## What changed

Moving from external review toward embedded evaluation. Over the past year METR reviewed several of Anthropic's Risk Reports; Anthropic also works with SecureBio and Accenture and is in early talks with others (including Hugging Face).

## Why independent evaluators

Anthropic cannot share all safety information publicly (some could aid malicious actors; some is commercially sensitive). Instead it gives third-party evaluators deep access and asks for evidence and reasoned conclusions, narrowing the gap between what evaluators and the company know. Effective evaluators need: **expertise** (including how evaluations fail, e.g. alignment faking); **independence** (no equity, no financial/operational dependence); **sufficient access** (latest models, training details, safeguard functioning); and **freedom to publish**. The analogy drawn is bank supervision with resident examiners inside large banks.

## Employee-like access with METR

Six METR employees onboarded with company hardware, office space, and internal-tool access roughly comparable to Anthropic's own risk teams. Staff are encouraged to share information except: customer data, attorney-client privileged material, and model architecture details (high leak risk, low relevance; sharing needs explicit approval). METR will publish its analysis; Anthropic cannot veto publication but can require redaction of the predefined categories, and METR may comment on the access it received. The embedded team is small, under enforceable agreements, and separated from other METR staff.

## Access tiers

- **Tier 1:** narrowly scoped investigations; Anthropic decides access; publication needs approval.
- **Tier 2:** publish without approval, broader access; Anthropic can require removal of certain categories, but evaluators may state whether redactions were justified.
- **Tier 3:** the METR arrangement — employee-like access, publication rights similar to Tier 2.

SecureBio is reviewing the chem/bio weapons chapter of the August Risk Report; Anthropic is pursuing a broader partnership with Accenture and Faculty (evaluation, red-teaming, alignment assessments, safeguard testing, capability building).

## Why it matters

Anthropic frames embedded evaluation as a step toward robust safety oversight for the industry and argues every frontier company should voluntarily do the same.
