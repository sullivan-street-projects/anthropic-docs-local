# Our position on open-weights models

_By Dario Amodei, Anthropic CEO._

## Main Position

Anthropic categorically denies advocating for bans on open-weights models. Amodei states that Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models. He clarifies that open-weights models without dangerous capabilities serve as public goods, providing value without substantial cost beyond computational resources.

## Primary National Security Concerns

Amodei identifies two "nightmare scenarios" that shape Anthropic's policy positions:

1. **Authoritarian AI superiority:** Authoritarian governments—particularly China—developing AI models surpassing U.S. capabilities to achieve military dominance or enable domestic repression. This concern transcends whether models use open weights or remain proprietary.
2. **Misuse risks:** Powerful AI models weaponized for cyberattacks or biological attacks, compounded by alignment problems. Open-weights models present elevated risks because safeguards cannot be enforced post-release and monitoring becomes impossible.

## Supported Policy Measures

Rather than bans, Amodei advocates three targeted interventions:

**Chip export controls:** The most direct approach involves restricting semiconductor sales to China and enforcing against smuggling. He notes that without access to advanced chips, China's scaling limitations prevent it from building superior models.

**Distillation crackdowns:** Industrial-scale distillation operations—more compute-efficient than training from scratch—enable China to narrow the frontier gap. Targeted policy interventions should address state-backed distillation operations specifically, not open-weights models generally.

**Mandatory safety testing:** All sufficiently capable models, regardless of openness or origin, should undergo rigorous pre-release testing for cyber, biological, and alignment risks. This approach aligns with recent Trump administration initiatives and industry proposals.

## Response to Open Letter

Amodei agrees that open weights expand economic access and strengthen competition, but disputes claims about inherent safety advantages. He expresses concern about attacker-defender asymmetries, particularly in biological contexts: sufficiently capable models may be able to quickly weaponize pandemic-level viruses with widely available materials, whereas defense against these agents is a multi-year operational task.

## Summary

Anthropic opposes blanket bans while supporting chip restrictions, distillation oversight, and comprehensive safety-testing frameworks applicable to all frontier models.
