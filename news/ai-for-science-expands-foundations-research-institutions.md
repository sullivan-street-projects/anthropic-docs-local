# AI for Science expands to foundations and research institutions

- **Published:** October 28, 2026
- **Category:** Announcements
- **Topics:** Discovery, Research programs, Partnerships, Access, Life sciences

## Overview

- Anthropic is highlighting **20 scientific foundations, institutions, and research groups** it has partnered with as part of an expansion of its **AI for Science** program, which gives free Claude credits to researchers working on high-impact projects.
- The program launched in **May 2025**, based on the belief that AI could significantly speed up scientific progress.
- Anthropic also launched a **discounted Team plan for scientists** and has supported hundreds of projects across biology, medicine, physics, chemistry, climate science, and mathematics.
- Scientists report using Claude to generate hypotheses, design experiments, analyze complex data, and communicate their findings.
- **Highlighted partnerships:**
  - **HHMI Janelia Research Campus** worked with Anthropic on the **Model Hardware Standard (MHS)**, a shared specification that lets AI agents operate lab instruments safely. MHS is in research preview.
  - The **Allen Institute** is building teams of AI agents to analyze its large open datasets on the brain, cells, and immune system, aiming to turn months of manual work into hours.
- Many projects are expected to produce public goods such as open datasets, open-source software, open standards, and shared libraries.

## 01 Science funders and societies

These organizations receive Claude credits and will distribute them to researchers:

| Organization | Description |
|---|---|
| Alfred P. Sloan Foundation | Founded in 1934; supports research and education in STEM and economics |
| Simons Foundation | Advances research in mathematics and the basic sciences through grants and the Flatiron Institute |
| Schmidt Sciences | Founded in 2024 by Eric and Wendy Schmidt; priorities include AI and advanced computing, astrophysics, biosciences, climate, and space |
| Open Source for Science Fund (Renaissance Philanthropy) | Time-bound fund to sustain and evolve the open-source software stack that science depends on |
| US National Science Foundation | $9 billion federal agency established in 1950; provides ~25% of federal support for basic research at US colleges and universities |
| CAS, a division of the American Chemical Society | Has indexed published chemistry for over a century; maintains the CAS REGISTRY, the standard for identifying chemical substances |

| Institution | Description |

## 03 Research projects and initiatives

These projects receive Claude credits directly:

| Project | Host / field | Description |
|---|---|---|
| FlyWire | Princeton University; neuroscience | Produced the first complete wiring map of an adult fruit fly brain; develops open connectomics tools |
| LEAP | Columbia University; Earth system science | NSF center combining machine learning and physics to improve climate models of clouds, oceans, and land |
| AstroPath | Johns Hopkins University; cancer research | Applies astronomical survey calibration methods to image tumors cell by cell, with consistent measurements across slides and labs |
| gnomAD | Broad Institute; genomics and rare disease | Open reference of human genetic variation built from DNA data from over 800,000 people |
| Tau Ceti | Mathematics | Open, community-driven project building a shared autoformalized mathematics library in Lean |
| Centre for Population Genomics | Garvan Institute of Medical Research and Murdoch Children's Research Institute (Australia) | Works to include multicultural communities in genomic resources and to build rare disease diagnostic tools |

## 04 What's next

- Many of the collaborations are at an early stage. Anthropic expects to share their impact and the new ways of working they enable in these fields.
- The AI for Science program will continue supporting scientists, institutions, and foundations, and plans to expand to more partners worldwide.
