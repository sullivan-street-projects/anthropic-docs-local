# Working at the frontier shapes expectations for the future

Anthropic Economics, **October 16, 2026**. An overview post introducing two companion reports — [How employment is changing at Anthropic](employment-at-anthropic.md) and [The AI expectations gap](ai-expectations-gap.md).

## Thesis

Anthropic staff and US workers do not disagree about the basic economics of AI and jobs; they differ in their *experience* of what AI can currently do, and that difference shapes their views of the future. As a company at the frontier of AI adoption, Anthropic may preview broader changes in how work is organized, how companies hire, and where labor demand goes.

## Part one: AI inside Anthropic

Over the past year Anthropic hired a smaller share of junior staff and a smaller share now work in technical roles, while agent work hours grew quickly: by March 2026 agents worked more hours than employees, and by August there were ~6× more agent hours than employee hours, with productivity rising (more code per person — an imperfect metric — more sales closed per rep, more contracts processed). Tracking individuals over time, output rose as they adopted AI more fully. The main constraint is now human attention, so employees increasingly build tools to monitor their agents.

## Part two: the expectations gap

Comparing Anthropic staff to ~11,000 US adults (Morning Consult, August, weighted): both expect rapid progress, but staff expect it faster. Nobel-level AI discovery by 2030: 39% of adults vs 91% of staff. AI doing 80%+ of one's current work by 2030: >50% of staff vs ~5% of Americans. Within-group variation is large (roughly two in ten staff hold views like a typical American). On economic questions the groups largely agree — forecasts diverge because personal experience with AI differs.

## Why it matters

Frontline experience with AI tools shapes beliefs about the future more than disagreement over economic theory does. If Anthropic's workplace is an early indicator, fewer junior hires, rising agent workloads, and a growing need to oversee AI work may foreshadow shifts elsewhere.
