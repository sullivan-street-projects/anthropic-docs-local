# Scenarios for our Economic Future

**Version:** Econ Scenario Explorer v1.0, September 2026
**Authors:** Anton Korinek, Chad Jones, Szymon Sacher, Tess Cotter, and Peter McCrory built the model and co-wrote the report. Santi Ruiz wrote the web piece.
**Companion report:** [*Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI* (Korinek et al., 2026)](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/cf58f84d46a4a76bf5a5b039ac695fba6b80041c.pdf)

This interactive explorer from Anthropic's Economics team models how AI could affect US jobs, growth, wages and unemployment through 2030. Users enter predictions about AI capabilities, adoption, autonomy, productivity and adjustment time, and the explorer shows the economy those predictions imply.

## Method: the economy is made of tasks

- Jobs are bundles of tasks, taken from the US Department of Labor's O\*NET taxonomy.
- Each task can stay unchanged, be **augmented** (AI helps a human), be **automated**, or be joined by **new AI-created tasks**. In a nurse's job, for example, AI might draft discharge instructions (augmented) and chart vitals (automated), while bathing a patient stays human-only.
- Adding up all task instances gives the US economy, which the page puts at over $30 trillion a year.

## Three scenarios

- **Modest:** AI's impact looks like the internet's. The gains are real, but they arrive gradually and stay within historical norms.
- **Substantial:** By 2030, AI can do half of all knowledge work, mostly autonomously, though most knowledge tasks are still done without it. The economy grows at twice its normal rate. Knowledge-worker wages stay flat, while other workers gain.
- **Extreme:** AI beats humans at most knowledge tasks, does nearly all of them autonomously, and creates almost no new knowledge tasks for people. This likely requires recursive self-improvement and fast adoption. GDP grows 15% a year, doubling the economy every 4.5 years, and unemployment rises past typical recession levels.

## Findings

1. **GDP:** AI grows the economy in every scenario, by very different amounts.
2. **Reallocation:** In the substantial and extreme scenarios, more knowledge workers (coders, call-center agents) have to move into less-exposed jobs (electricians, nurses). Prolonged unemployment is a risk, especially in the extreme case.
3. **Wages:** Average wages rise, but the gains land mostly outside knowledge work. Knowledge-worker wages are flat in the substantial scenario and fall more than 10% by 2030 in the extreme one.
4. **Labor vs. capital:** More of the growth can go to capital. In the extreme scenario, total labor income barely changes by 2030. The page concludes that "the main challenge is not achieving economic growth" but making sure the benefits are broadly shared.

## Public survey

In August, Anthropic surveyed 10,980 members of the general public and 32,153 site visitors about AI capabilities, adoption, autonomy, productivity, and how long it takes to find a new job. The typical respondent's answers imply the **substantial** scenario: GDP about 10% higher by 2030 than without AI, and unemployment around 5%. About 10% of respondents hold views consistent with the extreme scenario.

## Limitations (v1.0)

The model leaves out policy responses, business cycles, aggregate-demand effects (including the data-center buildout), financial-market disruptions, catastrophic risks and hyper-capable robots. It does not track individual workers. External reviewers, including Daron Acemoglu, David Autor and Ben Jones, commented on an early draft but did not endorse the conclusions. In the page's words, "actual outcomes may differ materially."

## Purpose

The work will inform Anthropic-funded research on labor-market interventions, and on policy proposals for sharing AI's economic benefits broadly in the US and globally. The page stresses that "the future is not predetermined."
