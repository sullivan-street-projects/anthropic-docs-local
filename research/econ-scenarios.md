# Scenarios for Our Economic Future

**Source:** Anthropic Economics Team
**Date:** September 2026 (v1.0)

## Overview

Anthropic's Economics team developed a model exploring how AI might reshape the US economy through 2030, examining potential impacts on jobs, growth, and unemployment across three distinct scenarios.

## Key Framework: The Task-Based Model

The analysis breaks down all economic work into component tasks. AI can affect these tasks in four ways:

- **Unchanged:** Tasks AI cannot perform (e.g., bathing patients)
- **Augmented:** AI helps workers perform tasks better or faster
- **Automated:** AI completes tasks independently
- **New tasks created:** Historical precedent shows technology generates new work

## Three Economic Scenarios

### Modest Scenario

AI delivers economic impact comparable to the internet—real gains within historical norms arriving gradually. GDP grows 1.6%, reaching $34.1 trillion.

### Substantial Scenario

"AI is capable of doing half of all knowledge work by 2030, the majority of it autonomously," yet most knowledge tasks remain human-performed. GDP grows 8.3% to $36.3 trillion. Knowledge worker wages stagnate while other workers see gains.

### Extreme Scenario

AI surpasses human capability across most knowledge work, completed nearly autonomously with minimal new knowledge tasks created. GDP reaches $44.4 trillion (32.4% growth), requiring likely recursively self-improving AI with rapid adoption.

## Critical Findings

**Finding 1 - GDP Growth:** All scenarios produce economic expansion, but distribution varies dramatically depending on AI capabilities and adoption rates.

**Finding 2 - Job Reallocation:** Substantial and extreme scenarios require significant occupational switching. Knowledge workers may transition to fields like electrician or nursing. In extreme scenarios, unemployment could reach historic recession levels.

**Finding 3 - Wages:** "Average wages rise...concentrated in occupations outside knowledge work." Knowledge worker wages stagnate or decline in transformative scenarios.

**Finding 4 - Labor vs. Capital Share:** Critical finding—while economies grow, larger portions flow to capital ownership rather than workers. In extreme scenarios, labor's share drops from ~60% to 45.2%, while capital's rises to 54.8%.

## Public Opinion Data

A survey of 10,980+ Americans revealed typical respondents expect outcomes aligning with the substantial scenario: 10% higher GDP by 2030 and ~5% unemployment, with approximately 10% holding extreme scenario expectations.

## Implications for Policy

Anthropic emphasizes that "the main challenge is not achieving economic growth, but making sure the benefits are broadly shared and the costs aren't unequally dispersed," particularly in extreme scenarios where knowledge workers face significant wage pressure and employment disruption.

## Model Limitations

The framework explicitly excludes:

- Hyper-capable robotics scenarios
- Policy responses
- Business cycles
- Aggregate demand effects from data center buildout
- Individual worker trajectories

The developers acknowledge this represents "a stark simplification of a complex reality" and commit to evolving the model as evidence develops.
