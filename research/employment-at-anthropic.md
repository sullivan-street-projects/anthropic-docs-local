# How employment is changing at Anthropic

Anthropic Economics (Wilmers, Catipon, Massenkoff, McCrory), **October 16, 2026**. How AI has reshaped work inside the company — which, having built its workflows heavily around AI, may preview broader labor-market shifts.

## Data and methods

Internal records on headcount, AI usage, output metrics, employee surveys, Slack/communication patterns, calendar data, and hiring scorecards, plus 17 employee interviews. To isolate AI's productivity effect, a staggered-adoption design follows 192 engineers who adopted coding agents vs 74 who did not (person and month fixed effects). Caveat: adoption is not random (high-output projects may self-select).

## Hiring

Anthropic grew fast but hired *less* per dollar of revenue than Meta or Google at a similar age (revenue per employee 2–4× higher). Junior-level staff fell from 21% to 14% of technical departments (early 2025 → mid-2026); junior share of technical *hires* fell from 27% to 9%. Technical share of total employment dropped from 61% to 56% as sales/finance/recruiting grew faster.

## Productivity

By March 2026 agent work hours surpassed human work hours; by August, agents logged **six hours per human hour** in employee-directed sessions. Code commits per engineer reached ~5× their level six months earlier. No difference in the rate at which agent-written vs human-written lines were later deleted/reverted. Sales reps who adopted agents closed more deals (one talked to 30% more customers); lawyers and AP staff processed more work.

## Human bottlenecks

~80% of interactive agent session time is idle, waiting for human input. For non-technical groups, reading all interactive Claude output would take over half a work week. "Meta-work" (building pipelines, monitoring, standing instructions) grew from 10% to 22% of interactive sessions; median meeting time rose 20% YoY.

## Hiring criteria

Scorecards increasingly cite candidates' creative use of AI tools and emphasize autonomy and judgment over domain expertise and execution.

## Policy implications

Three features worth considering: broad employee stock ownership (incentive alignment); an early-warning system flagging roles facing disruption with internal reallocation; and caution about "so-so" automation that cuts labor cost with little productivity gain. These do not address the junior-hiring slowdown, which the authors say calls for retraining and income-support policy. Anthropic is unusual, so findings may not generalize.
