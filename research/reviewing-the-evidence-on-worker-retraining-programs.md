# Reviewing the Evidence on Worker Retraining Programs

## Overview

Anthropic's Economic Research team released a comprehensive evidence review examining the effectiveness of worker retraining programs as a policy response to labor market disruption from AI. The report was coauthored by independent researcher David Roodman and Anthropic's Maxim Massenkoff.

## Key Findings

### General Program Effectiveness

The analysis synthesized findings from 56 randomized U.S. studies plus European experimental evidence. The results indicate that training programs produce positive but modest effects: for each person offered a training slot, employment rises by two to three percentage points and earnings by roughly $1,000 a year, against a cost of about $13,000.

Government cost-recovery is limited—the state recovers more than half of what it spends, and programs roughly break even overall when accounting for tax revenue and reduced benefit payments.

### High-Performing Programs

A specialized subset called "sector programs" shows promise. These initiatives partner with employers in a high-demand industry and place people directly into jobs in that field, producing gains several times larger than standard programs. However, replication efforts have frequently failed.

## Methodology

The research employed meta-analysis techniques combining multiple randomized controlled trials with international evidence.

## Conclusions and Recommendations

The authors conclude that existing retraining infrastructure would likely prove insufficient if AI causes large-scale worker displacement. They recommend immediate investment in demonstrating, evaluating, and scaling promising programs, particularly through rapid expansion of leading models with rigorous outcome measurement. Anthropic established its Economic Futures Research Fund to support such investigations.
