# Agentic Coding and Persistent Returns to Expertise

**Publication Date:** June 16, 2026

## Overview

Anthropic researchers analyzed approximately 400,000 Claude Code sessions from October 2025 through April 2026 to understand how AI-assisted coding tools affect work patterns and outcomes across different skill levels and occupations.

## Key Findings

**Division of Labor**
The research reveals a clear separation of responsibilities: "people make most of the planning decisions (what to do) and Claude makes most of the execution decisions (how to do it)." Users contribute roughly 70% of planning decisions while handling only 20% of execution decisions.

**Expertise Impact**
Domain expertise significantly influences tool effectiveness. Expert-level users trigger substantially more Claude activity per instruction -- approximately 12 actions and 3,200 words of output compared to 5 actions and 600 words for novice users.

**Success Rates**
Success correlates strongly with user expertise rather than job title. Novice sessions achieve verified success in 15% of cases versus 28-33% for intermediate and expert users. Notably, "every one of the ten largest occupations lands within seven points of software engineers" on coding success rates.

**Work Composition Changes**
Between October and April, debugging sessions declined from 33% to 19% of activity, while operational tasks and data analysis doubled from roughly 10% to 20%.

**Task Value Growth**
Average session values increased approximately 25% over the seven-month period, with building tasks rising about 43% and operating tasks increasing 34%.

## Implications

The findings suggest agentic coding tools reward domain knowledge over formal programming training, potentially democratizing technical work while maintaining advantages for those with subject-matter expertise.
