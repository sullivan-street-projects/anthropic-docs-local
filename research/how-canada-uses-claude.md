# How Canada Uses Claude: Findings from the Anthropic Economic Index

## Overview

This report analyzes Claude adoption patterns across Canada using data from the Anthropic Economic Index, based on a sample of Claude.ai conversations from February 2026.

## Key Findings

### Global and National Adoption

Canada represents 2.6% of global Claude.ai traffic and ranks 8th worldwide by volume. Its adoption index of 4.4 indicates usage per capita is more than four times higher than would be expected relative to population size. Among the top ten countries, Canada ranks second only to the United States in per-capita usage.

### Regional Concentration

Adoption is heavily concentrated geographically. Ontario accounts for 43.9% of Canadian conversations, while Ontario, Quebec, British Columbia, and Alberta combined represent approximately 94% of national usage. Per capita, British Columbia leads at 1.4x expected levels, followed by Ontario at 1.1x. Other provinces fall below expected adoption rates, with Newfoundland and Labrador at only 0.2x.

### Workforce Composition as Key Driver

Unlike global patterns where income correlates with adoption, provincial disparities in Canada align more closely with economic structure. Regions with larger professional, scientific, and technical services sectors show systematically higher usage, suggesting that model capabilities well-matched to the composition of local economies primarily determine adoption rates.

### Consistent Usage Patterns

Across all provinces, usage remains relatively uniform: personal use comprises 44-51% of conversations, work-related use represents 34-40%, and coursework accounts for 13-18%.

### Distinctive Canadian Use Cases

Translation requests correlate with public administration employment, reflecting Canada's official bilingualism policy. Document translation is the most distinctive Canadian use case compared to Australian, British, and American peers. Canadian usage tilts toward education and early-career support, with academic coursework, coding assistance, and resume drafting overrepresented relative to peer nations.

## Conclusion

Canada's high Claude adoption reflects both its advanced-economy status and its distinctive workforce composition. Rather than income alone, regional adoption patterns align with concentrations in professional and technical services sectors, consistent with earlier findings regarding adoption diffusion within the United States.
