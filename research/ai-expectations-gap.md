# The AI expectations gap

Anthropic Economics, **October 16, 2026**.

## Central thesis

The public and frontier AI developers largely agree about what AI can do *today* but diverge sharply on how fast it will improve, how widely it will be adopted, and how much it will change their own jobs. The disagreement is not about labor-market theory — people who expect AI to do more of their own work forecast higher unemployment in both groups, drawing the link similarly. The gap is mainly about differing experience and expectations of AI capability.

## Method and data

Two surveys in August 2026: a nationally representative, weighted sample of **10,980 US adults** (Morning Consult, asked about 2027 or 2030) and an unweighted internal poll of **1,334 Anthropic staff**. A September replication (7,613 adults) produced similar results. Answers were fed into a scenario model to estimate implied GDP growth; 23,075 website-quiz visitors served as a middle reference.

## Key findings

- **Capabilities:** 39% of adults vs 91% of staff expect a Nobel-level AI discovery by 2030. 19–40% of adults say AI will *never* perform a given task, vs ≤2% of staff. Regular AI users are more optimistic than non-users but still below staff.
- **Adoption:** Adults expect AI used for 42% of tasks it could perform by 2030; staff expect 65%. Top obstacle adults named: customer preference for a human, then privacy, reliability, regulation.
- **Own jobs:** Staff estimate AI will handle 75% of their current work hours by 2030; US workers average 28% (only ~5% say 80%+).
- **Unemployment:** Workers expecting AI to handle 80%+ of their work forecast 12.2% unemployment in 2030; each +10 pts of exposed work ≈ +0.4 pts forecast unemployment (same pattern in staff).
- **Re-employment:** Median staff expect a displaced worker needs 12 months to find work in a new occupation; median adult 7–12 months.
- **Economic scenarios:** Median adult answers imply 5.4% GDP growth in 2030; median staff 17.1%; visitor median 12.5%.
- **Inside the lab:** Staff estimate AI already performs a median 51% of the work of building a frontier model, rising to 80% by end of 2027 and 95% by 2030.

## Why it matters

Policy and business planning based on public sentiment may underestimate how quickly AI could reshape work. Public expectations may shift as use spreads (regular users already sit closer to staff views). The staff figures are personal views from an internal poll, not company forecasts.
