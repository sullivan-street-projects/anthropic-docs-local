# Anthropic Economic Index Report: Cadences (June 26, 2026)

## Overview

This report analyzes Claude usage patterns and AI's economic impact through improved data collection methods, artifact classification, and survey data linking usage to user perceptions.

## Key Sections

### Chapter 1: Cadences (Usage Patterns)

**Workweek Patterns:**
Personal conversations spike from approximately 35% on weekdays to under 50% on weekends. Weekend Claude Code usage shifts toward AI agent design, quant trading, and gaming, while backend and debugging tasks decline.

**Daily Rhythms:**

- News requests peak at 7 a.m. local time
- Recipe requests are 2.3x more frequent at 6 p.m.
- Sleep advice peaks around dawn hours
- Business correspondence traces typical workday patterns

**Occupational Differences:**
During nights and weekends, work-related tasks skew toward higher-wage occupations. The report notes that "tasks related to jobs in the bottom two quartiles...fall to a smaller share of total conversations" during non-traditional hours.

**Tax Day Effect:**
Tax-related conversations spiked dramatically around the April 15 U.S. filing deadline, reaching eight times the average frequency on April 14.

### Chapter 2: Artifacts (Outputs)

**Output Categories:**

- Explanations: 17% of conversations
- Documents and reports: 15%
- Guidance: 11%
- 93% of conversations produce identifiable artifacts

**Work vs. Personal Use:**
Different artifacts serve different purposes. Marketing content creation is 80% work-related, while creative writing exceeds 80% personal use. Planning tasks split roughly evenly between work and personal applications.

**Computational Costs:**
Higher-wage occupations consume more tokens, suggesting "compute tends to scale with the value of the work." Marketing managers' tasks use approximately 2.5x more tokens than editors' tasks, correlating with wage differentials.

**AI Autonomy:**
Claude Code sessions show significantly higher autonomy levels than chat/Cowork across almost all output types. Blog posts average 13 back-and-forth turns in chat versus a single prompt in Claude Code, despite similar underlying tasks.

**Reading Level:**
Claude's responses average approximately one year higher education requirement than user prompts. The largest gaps appear in creative outputs like apps and games (+1.7 to +2.6 years).

### Chapter 3: Perceptions (Survey Findings)

**Survey Methodology:**
Approximately 9,700 Claude users linked usage data to survey responses using privacy-preserving methods. Respondents skew heavily toward computer and mathematical occupations (30% versus 4% of U.S. employment).

**Capability Expectations:**

- Close to 60% expect AI to handle larger task shares within 12 months
- Over one-third anticipate AI managing most of their work tasks
- Expectations for progress are "strikingly uniform" across occupations

**Geographic and Experience Effects:**
Reported AI exposure is negatively correlated with country GDP -- higher-income country workers report lower current capabilities. Experienced workers estimate lower current exposure but expect similar annual progress as early-career colleagues.

**Job Security Concerns:**
Approximately 10% rated job loss as likely or very likely -- slightly below baseline U.S. separation rates. Respondents expressed more concern about junior colleagues than themselves.

**Paradoxical Sentiment:**
Surprisingly, users engaging in more automated Claude sessions report greater optimism about future pay, job security, and skill value. The report suggests this could reflect either selection bias or tangible benefits from delegation.

**Gender Differences:**
Women comprise 12% of the linked sample and show distinctly different patterns: lower Claude Code usage (0.24 standard deviations), lower automation share (0.33 standard deviations), but higher total active chat time, indicating "more collaborative engagement."

**Economic Hopes:**
When asked about an AI-transformed economy in 10 years, the most common themes were:

- Human-AI collaboration on meaningful work (50%+ of respondents)
- Automation of tedious tasks for increased free time (50%+)
- Broad distribution of economic gains (~33%)

## Methodological Improvements

The report implements three key changes:

1. Higher sampling rates enabling hourly-level usage analysis
2. New classifier labeling conversation outputs
3. Granular monthly breakdowns for chat, Cowork, and API separately

## Discussion

The authors emphasize that AI's "earliest impacts" likely occur "in areas where it does the most work," making usage pattern analysis critical for understanding economic transformation.
