# Claude-shaped science

Anthropic (guest post), **October 1, 2026**. By Prof. Matthew Schwartz (visiting researcher at Anthropic during the project).

**Thesis:** Current models are capable but not yet good at working like human scientists — an "impedance mismatch." The fix is to find "Claude-shaped" problems suited to agentic AI's strengths, paired with domain experts who judge what's worth pursuing.

- **BootLoops:** an open-source toolkit (harness around any model) for exact calculations in quantitative science.
- **Strengths:** breadth of knowledge, coding, fast parsing of papers/data; weak at deep conceptual questions.
- **Physics origin:** on the semi-numerical S-matrix bootstrap, Claude reproduced published results and computed 15 never-before-computed integrals (of 30).
- **Cross-field spread:** ecology, population genetics, phylogenetics, economics, linguistics, earth science, genomics, astrophysics, statistics, cosmology. Example results: ecology (Barro Colorado Island tree mix changes 4.5x faster than neutral theory allows); population genetics (5.7B mutation pairs, evidence for gene conversion); economics (ported replication packages for 4,452 papers); linguistics (AccStack covers stress in 6,072 languages); solved Watson's 3D random-walk "final problem."
- **Operational lessons (36 manuscripts, 18 fields, ~400 candidate problems, 3 months):** Claude declares victory prematurely, gives unreliable time estimates, grinds through long calculations instead of building faster tools, loses context through compaction, and needs humans to check plots and supply judgment.

**Outlook:** AI accelerates the data-acquisition and iterative-checking loop without collapsing it; harnesses like BootLoops can fill gaps in the "convex hull" of human knowledge. Raises concerns about crediting contributors and training graduate students.
