# India Country Brief: The Anthropic Economic Index

## Executive Summary

India represents the world's second-largest market for Claude.ai usage, accounting for 5.8% of global conversations. Despite this substantial absolute volume, per-capita adoption remains modest when adjusted for working-age population, ranking 101st globally. The research reveals Indian users apply AI to complex professional tasks with remarkable efficiency.

## Key Findings

### Global AI Adoption Standing

India leads globally in absolute Claude.ai usage second only to the United States. However, this reflects population scale rather than widespread individual adoption. The per-capita usage gap suggests significant expansion potential beyond current concentrated user bases.

### Geographic and Occupational Concentration

Current AI adoption clusters in four economically dominant states—Maharashtra (15.5%), Tamil Nadu (13.2%), Karnataka (12.7%), and Delhi (10.5%)—mirroring India's established IT sector geography. Software development comprises 45.2% of tasks, the highest proportion internationally.

### Economic Primitives: Distinctive Usage Patterns

Indian users demonstrate distinctive AI collaboration characteristics:

- **Productivity acceleration**: Tasks requiring 3.8 hours complete in approximately 15 minutes—a 15x speedup versus 12x globally
- **Work emphasis**: 51.3% work-related usage compared to 46% globally
- **AI autonomy preference**: Users grant greater decision-making authority (3.60 versus 3.38 on delegation scales)
- **Complex task focus**: 84.6% of tasks exceed independent human capability

## Strategic Implications

Expanding AI's economic footprint requires extending adoption beyond technology sectors. Structural barriers including income inequality and digital infrastructure disparities currently limit broader penetration. Investment in AI literacy training could substantially enhance returns from expanded deployment.

Human prompt quality directly correlates with response sophistication—indicating that skill development programs targeting non-IT workforces may unlock significant productivity improvements.
