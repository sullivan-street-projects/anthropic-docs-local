# India Country Brief: The Anthropic Economic Index

## Executive Summary

India represents the world's second-largest market for Claude.ai usage, accounting for "5.8% of total Claude.ai use, second only to the United States." However, this substantial volume masks significant concentration disparities. The research reveals that Indian users predominantly work on complex professional tasks, delegating considerable autonomy to AI systems. The data suggests Indian professionals are applying AI at technological frontiers, tackling problems beyond typical human capability.

## Key Findings

### Global AI Adoption Rankings

India ranks second by absolute usage volume but dramatically lower by per-capita metrics. "On a per-capita basis, adjusting for the working-age population, India ranks 101st out of 116 countries with sufficient observation volume." This disparity reflects population size rather than widespread adoption.

### Geographic Concentration

Four states dominate usage: Maharashtra (15.5%), Tamil Nadu (13.2%), Karnataka (12.7%), and Delhi (10.5%). This concentration mirrors India's established IT sector geography, suggesting adoption remains rooted in existing technology hubs rather than broader economic sectors.

### Occupational Focus

Software development overwhelmingly leads usage patterns. "India ranks 1st globally in the share of AI use devoted to software-related tasks (45.2% of all O*NET-mapped tasks)." Educational applications represent the second-most common use case.

## Economic Primitives: Distinctive Usage Patterns

### Productivity Impact

Indian users compress tasks taking 3.8 hours into approximately 15 minutes—a "15x speedup" compared to the global "12x speedup." This suggests AI delivers outsized benefits for complex work.

### Work Orientation

Professional usage dominates at 51.3%, compared to 46% globally. Personal applications represent only 27.8% versus 34.7% internationally.

### AI Autonomy Levels

Indian users delegate significantly more decision-making authority to AI systems (3.60 on a 1-5 scale versus 3.38 globally), indicating greater willingness for independent AI operation.

### Task Capability

Only 84.6% of Indian tasks could theoretically be completed by humans alone, compared to 87.9% globally. This suggests Indian users frequently bring AI problems they cannot independently solve.

## Strategic Implications

**Sector Expansion Requirements**: Current adoption remains heavily weighted toward software occupations, limiting broader economic transformation potential.

**Infrastructure Dependencies**: Per-capita adoption gaps correlate with income disparities and digital infrastructure limitations.

**Skills Development**: "The strong correlation between prompt sophistication and response quality in the global data suggests that training programs focused on effective AI use...could meaningfully improve the returns from wider AI adoption."

**Autonomy Benefits**: Higher delegation levels combined with extended baseline task times indicate Indian professionals successfully leverage AI for capability enhancement.

## Methodology

Analysis draws from privacy-preserving Claude.ai consumer data spanning November 13-20, 2025, encompassing approximately 1 million conversations. Geographic assignment utilized IP-based geolocation; occupational classification mapped to O*NET taxonomies and Standard Occupational Classification systems.
