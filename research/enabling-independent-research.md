# Enabling Independent Research on How People Use Claude

## Overview

Anthropic piloted a program granting external researchers access to aggregate Claude usage data through Anthropic Insights, a privacy-preserving analysis tool. Three research institutions conducted independent studies on approximately 250,000 Claude conversations from April-May 2026, designing their own research questions without company interference over findings.

## Key Findings

### Stanford's Social and Language Technologies Lab — Human-AI Collaboration

- Over half of conversations involved delegating high-stakes work, contrary to expectations that people reserve consequential tasks for themselves.
- In roughly 75% of interactions, people set the direction while Claude assisted, and they usually adapted its output rather than using it verbatim.
- Friction during collaboration often proved productive, pushing users to clarify intentions and refine outputs.

### University of Oxford's Human Information Processing Lab — User Emotional Experiences

- Behavioral patterns correlated: warm Claude responses aligned with positive user sentiment; refusals corresponded with pushback.
- User engagement patterns resembled those observed in general web browsing studies.

### METR — Coding Productivity

- Newer Claude models delivered significant speedups over older models for task completion.
- Claude's time estimates reasonably predicted actual developer completion times.

## Methodology

Researchers never accessed raw conversations. Instead, they formulated questions that Claude answered across the dataset, with responses aggregated into categories showing percentage distributions. External partners tested questions on WildChat (a public dataset) before deployment on actual Claude data.

## Challenges and Solutions

The program proved resource-intensive and slow compared to standard research timelines. Question wording significantly affected categorization accuracy. Anthropic provided interpretive guidance and explored better question-development processes for future iterations.

## Privacy and Independence

Anthropic limited contractual review to user privacy, policy violations, confidential information, and research accuracy—granting researchers full publication independence. A third-party privacy audit by Imperial College London verified protections. Less than 5% of data required alteration due to policy violations.

## Future Directions

Anthropic plans to scale the program while maintaining privacy and quality standards. The company released aggregate datasets publicly on Hugging Face and solicits researcher interest through an expression-of-interest form.
