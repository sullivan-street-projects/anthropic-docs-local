# What work can robots do?

Anthropic Economics, **September 30, 2026**. By Russell Legate-Yang and Maxim Massenkoff.

- **Broad but shallow:** robots can perform ~74% of US physical tasks (34% of all working hours), mostly only in limited controlled settings.
- **Exposure tiers:** E0 (cannot), E1 (purpose-built robotic settings), E2 (structured human workplaces), E3 (unstructured environments).
- **Most exposed:** driving and warehouse jobs; taxi drivers highest (2.2/3); 9 of 10 most-exposed are vehicle operators. Nursing and repair much less exposed.
- **Robots + LLMs:** ~80% of job tasks by working time are exposed to robots or LLMs (LLMs alone ~half, rising to 81% with robots).
- **Cost is the main barrier:** robots are cost-competitive with human labor for only 0.3% of job tasks; at ~3%/yr price declines, reaching 10% takes ~40 years. Capability gaps block ~70% of physical tasks (manipulation most common); human preferences limit ~25%, regulation ~14%.
- **Backtest (1977–today):** jobs more exposed to existing robots saw larger wage/employment declines; robots gained ~2% of previously impossible physical work per year.
- **Examples:** packers/packagers (~560k workers, employment −22% since 2015); welders (~5x more expensive than humans).

**Takeaways:** Physical automation reaches driving/warehousing before nursing/repair; cost, dexterity, and regulation set the pace; baseline trends put half of physical work below cost-parity until ~2085 (fast-adoption ~2050).
