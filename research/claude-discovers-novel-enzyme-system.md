# Claude Discovers a Novel Enzyme System with CRISPR-like Repeats

Published **September 23, 2026**. Anthropic's newly formed life sciences research group used Claude to identify a previously uncharacterized bacterial-phage enzyme system, termed **array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART)**.

## The discovery

In spring 2026, Claude identified a system found in bacteriophages (viruses that infect bacteria) with three components:

1. A reverse transcriptase enzyme
2. An associated partner gene
3. An array of evenly-spaced DNA repeat sequences resembling CRISPR arrays

## Methodology

The team tasked Claude with searching massive DNA sequence databases for interesting reverse transcriptase examples. Over roughly **21 hours, ~950 Claude agents analyzed ~200,000 reverse transcriptases**, identified ~3,500 candidate systems, and narrowed to ~20 compelling candidates. One agent detected the distinctive repeat pattern adjacent to an unusual reverse transcriptase — the kind of anomaly hunt that typically takes human scientists weeks or months.

## Why it matters

The finding is placed in a lineage of biological discoveries that began by noticing unusual molecular patterns: restriction enzymes (biotech), Taq polymerase (PCR), and CRISPR (gene editing). ART's primary function is still unknown, but its characteristics parallel programmable DNA-manipulation systems, suggesting possible therapeutic applications.

MIT CRISPR pioneer **Feng Zhang** endorsed the work as "an exciting example" of AI-driven biological discovery. The result demonstrates AI's capacity for autonomous anomaly detection and hypothesis generation in genomics.
